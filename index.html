Anku Rani

PhD Student · MIT Media Lab

I'm a PhD student at the MIT Media Lab, advised by Paul Pu Liang in the Multisensory Intelligence group and co-advised by Pattie Maes. I work at the intersection of multimodal AI and human-computer interaction. My research focuses on breaking health-related socio-cultural misinformation by building interactive multimodal systems like video agents that adapt across demographics to make medical information culturally resonant for the communities they reach.

I spent the summer of 2026 training LLMs at IBM Research with Rogerio Feris, and continue as a visiting student at the MIT–IBM Watson AI Lab. Before that I spent a summer at Adobe Research working on multimodal mathematical reasoning, and was a research specialist at the AI Institute, University of South Carolina, with Amit Sheth.

Research

Durably Reducing Belief in Women's Health Misinformation Through Culturally Adaptive AI Videos

A Rani, K Jaidka, S Sharma, P Mahajan, M Wadhwa, A Lippman, P Maes, P Liang

A field study with 434 women in suburban India. Presenters that matched a community's culture cut false health beliefs by 30% — roughly double a culturally neutral presenter — and the effect held three weeks later.

arXiv🤗dataset

CultureScore: Evaluating Cultural Faithfulness in Video Generation Models

A Rani, W Dai, S Nayak, P Maes, M Kalayeh, PP Liang

EMNLP 2026

A benchmark for whether video generation models depict a culture faithfully or fall back on stereotype.

arXiv🤗dataset

Dialogues with AI Reduce Beliefs in Misinformation but Build No Lasting Discernment Skills

A Rani, V Danry, PP Liang, A Lippman, P Maes

CHI 2026Best Paper Honorable Mention

Over four weeks, 67 participants judged news headlines and images. Chatbot help made them 21% more accurate in the moment, but built no discernment that survived once the help was taken away.

paperdata, code & materialsMIT News

RADAR: A Reasoning-Guided Attribution Framework for Explainable Visual Data Analysis

A Rani, A Garimella, A Saxena, B Srinivasan, PP Liang

EACL Findings 2026

An attribution mechanism that makes multimodal models show which parts of a chart actually support their answer, across visual question answering, reasoning, and attribution.

paper

SEPSIS: I Can Catch Your Lies — A New Paradigm for Deception Detection

A Rani, D Dalal, S Gautam, P Gupta, V Jain, A Chadha, A Sheth, A Das

ACL SRW 2025

Reframes deception around lies of omission — their types, colors, intentions and topics — with an NLP model built to catch them.

FACTIFY-5WQA: 5W Aspect-based Fact Verification through Question Answering

A Rani, SM Tonmoy, D Dalal, S Gautam, M Chakraborty, A Chadha, A Sheth, A Das

ACL 2023 Main Conference

Fact verification decomposed into Who, What, When, Where and Why, so a system can point to exactly where a claim and its evidence diverge.

Partisan and Geographic Variation in Emotional Responses to COVID-19 Vaccination on Social Media

K Jaidka, Y Wu, A Rani, O Kuru, R Jamalova, G Sherman, SC Guntuku

JAMA Network Open, 2026

County-level emotional response to the US COVID-19 vaccine rollout, and whether anger, anxiety and incivility in vaccine tweets predict local hesitancy and vaccination rates.

paper

Counter Turing Test (CT²): Investigating AI-Generated Text Detection for Hindi

I Kavathekar, A Rani, A Chamoli, P Kumaraguru, A Sheth, A Das

EMNLP Findings 2024

A benchmark and detectability index for AI-generated text in Hindi, ranking LLMs by how detectable their Hindi output is.
